# Team playbook: a tech lead's first 90 days with AI-assisted delivery

Scope: a small team on Effect TS, AWS Bedrock and LLM features shipping a healthcare consult-recording product ("Scribe"), led by someone four weeks in. Sources are named with dates; peer-reviewed and first-party-with-method sources are quoted numerically, vendor sources directionally. Bracketed tags such as `[R2.4]` are internal provenance labels; strip them before circulating.

## Step zero: diagnose before you tool

AI amplifies the system a team already has: adoption correlates with higher throughput and lower delivery stability in both large 2025-2026 datasets (DORA, 2025-09-24; Faros AI, 2026-04-12) [R6.1, R1.1]. Week one measures lead time from first commit to production and where work waits; then name the matching DORA team profile out loud, so "we are slow" becomes a diagnosis the team can argue with [R6.1]. Opinion (Watkins, The First 90 Days): write one sentence naming the situation you inherited and what would prove you wrong, and test it in weeks 5-8 against delivery data before any tooling push; "slow to ship" fits no outcome clarity, a broken pipeline, or a team defending a standard nobody told them how to meet [R6.19].

Gains arrive by quadrant: roughly 35-40% on greenfield low-complexity work, 0-10% on brownfield high-complexity, about half of gross gains returned as rework (Denisov-Blanch, Stanford, ~100k developers; secondary reporting of a talk) [R6.3, R1.11]. Mature Scribe paths sit in the worst quadrant, so agent-heavy work goes to new services and humans-plus-agent-review to the legacy consult-recording paths; if the backlog is mostly high-complexity brownfield, scope and batch size are the lever [R1.11]. No vendor number as a target: Shopify's ~20% is an executive's "humble estimate", and a missed 54% reads as the team failing [R1.2].

## 30-60-90

**Days 1-30: capture and subtract.**
- Day 1, three numbers: median PR size (lines, files), median time to first review, and a one-week hand tally of review comments bucketed style/naming, correctness, structure/design, question. The team predicts the day-90 shift first; developers misjudge their own speed by ~39 points (METR, 2025-07-10: forecast +24%, believed +20%, measured -19%) [R2.14].
- Formatter, lint and typecheck become required CI checks; style comments go out of scope; every checklist item a tool can decide is deleted [R2.8].
- Publish the review contract below in Google's wording [R2.18]; move the sit-down off the merge path and re-badge it as teaching [R2.6].
- Board items with owners: 10 December 2026, and the Effect v4 spike [R3.3, R3.8]. Circulate the AI stance page for comment, not announcement [R6.4].
- Establish what review record a healthcare consult-recording product must keep before optimising any review path for speed [R2.16].

**Days 31-60: change what reviewers are asked, not who they are.**
- The five-question PR template joins the checklist: problem, rejected alternatives, rationale for any pattern new to the codebase, what you ran, which hunks are AI-generated and unverified [R2.3, R2.12].
- One day moving duplication and error-masking into CI (jscpd, dependency-cruiser: unevaluated candidates) and settling the import-graph question with a tool; reuse and design stay human [R2.20].
- Stacked PRs above the size target, sold as "unblocks the author"; the cycle-time claims are vendor marketing [R2.4]. Review budgeted on the board; open PRs capped at 2-3 per author [R2.13].
- Repo CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md and a one-page "the Effect we use" land as reviewed PRs [R6.7, R3.9].
- The Bedrock call becomes a Layer with a test implementation, on plain Effect rather than `@effect/ai` (self-described experimental/alpha), with 14.ai's token-stream duplication feeding production traces to error analysis off the user path: ordinary tests for the AI bit, and the seam that makes residency enforceable [R3.7]. The Effect readability-certification analogue starts [R2.15].

**Days 61-90: prove and prune.** Re-run the tally and pickup time, prediction first [R2.14]. Audit any AI reviewer against the sub-10% effective-false-positive bar and switch it off if it fails [R2.8, R2.9]. Distil eight weeks of sit-down arguments into a five-page "how we build Scribe", and prune what did not earn its slot [R2.15, R2.16].

## The review contract (one page, every line attributed)

- Approve once the change "definitely improves the overall code health of the system being worked on, even if the CL isn't perfect"; prefix non-blocking polish with "Nit: " (Google, The Standard of Code Review). The approval rule is what makes nit-labelling stick [R2.18].
- First response within one business day is the ceiling (Google eng-practices); aim at hours, since Google's median for the whole review is under 4 hours (Sadowski et al., ICSE-SEIP 2018) [R2.5, R2.17].
- Size norm near 50 lines and 5 files, from Google's 24-line median; a size label and SLA, never a merge block; oversized diffs get bounced. The 400-line rule is one 2006 vendor case study in which 61% of reviews found zero defects [R2.4, R2.17, R1.4].
- One reviewer by default, and only correctness and structure block [R2.8]. A second is for familiarity, which roughly doubles acted-on comments (65-71% vs 32-37%, Bosu et al., MSR 2015): keep less experienced reviewers on and add one experienced one [R2.17, R2.2].
- An objection names a concrete failure mode or a named principle; authorship, tone and "this does not look like how we write" are not findings. An unknown pattern earns "explain it to me" plus a short writeup, not a change request [R6.10].
- A PR introducing a new pattern carries a "why this pattern" paragraph naming the alternative it beat; "understanding the reason of the change" is the top reviewer challenge (Bacchelli and Bird, ICSE 2013) [R2.7].
- Everyone, no names: you must be able to explain at review why the code does what it does, and "the agent chose that" is not an explanation. Pair on the explaining. The lead's call; DORA's version is junior-with-senior pairing on architectural decisions (DORA, 2026-03-10) [R6.11].
- Effect-form questions for what CI cannot decide: is the error channel typed rather than collapsed by a catch-all, are resources scoped, are retries and timeouts explicit [R2.11].
- AI first-pass reviewer, if one arrives: at most ~5 findings ranked by confidence, no approve or block authority, labelled non-consensus, fed a curated diff plus the author's summary rather than the repository, told that zero findings is the expected result on a correct diff, re-tested on ten real Scribe PRs first. Frontier reviewers detect 15-31% of human-flagged issues diff-only and degrade as context grows (SWE-PRBench, 2026-03-27); LLM reviewers over-reject correct code at false-negative rates of 26-88%, worse with "explain and propose a fix" scaffolding (Jin and Chen, Automated Software Engineering, 2026) [R2.9, R2.10, R6.9].

## The AI stance page (outline)

A clear, communicated AI stance is one of DORA's seven amplifying capabilities and the cheapest: "ambiguity creates risk; a clear policy provides the psychological safety needed for effective experimentation" (dora.dev/capabilities) [R6.4].

1. What agents may touch and may not, given PHI in Scribe [R6.4]. Nobody is measured on AI usage, and not using it on a task is legitimate [R6.4].
2. Agent-authored PRs get a human approver, always, in Scribe; anything unattended stays where a bad merge is cheap and no PHI is in scope. Anthropic and Shopify keep a human approver; the one autonomous gate on record (Cloudflare, 2026-04-20) runs on its own infrastructure repos [R1.16, R1.5]. Volunteer this line before anyone asks.
3. Gains by quadrant with the rework caveat, cited as secondary reporting [R6.3], and the measurement stance below, including what will not be measured [R6.5].
4. The dip: DORA's ROI work (2026-04-22, via InfoQ 2026-05-11) models a J-curve from workflow adaptation, verification burden and process adjustment; state the review date, and use the ROI arithmetic only to open a conversation with management [R6.14].
5. Every rule paired with its mechanism: hook or CI check for must-never-happen, an outcome condition re-checked each turn, a fresh-context review pass before human review, prose only for what cannot be checked. Instructions are advisory and hooks deterministic, but a Stop hook is a nudge the CLI overrides after 8 consecutive blocks, so CI is the only hard gate [R6.6, R6.8].
6. A no-stakes disclosure channel seeded by the lead's own failures; "I generated this and here is how I checked it" as a normal PR sentence. 57% of workers hid AI use in a 47-country survey (KPMG and University of Melbourne, fieldwork to Jan 2025): a reason to write the policy, not a claim about this team [R6.15].
7. A work-in-progress cap and one line of acceptance criteria per ticket, verified by the author before review (the lead's judgement, not DORA's) [R6.18].

## Measurement stance

- Team level only: no individual usage tracking, no AI-authorship percentage, no acceptance rate. Publish what will not be tracked; that removes the incentive to perform usage (DX, 2025-11-12, vendor; corroborated by DORA, 2026-03-10) [R6.5]. Scoreboard: delivery lead time, change failure rate, review latency, one product outcome; first honest read 3-6 months out [R6.5].
- "PRs merged without review" as the leading indicator (Faros +31.3%, directional), with a stability counterpart beside every throughput number: revert or fix-forward rate and rework per landed PR [R1.3, R1.1]. GitClear carries quality decay numerically (623M changes: duplication +81%, refactoring -70%, error-masking +47%) and Faros directionally; they measure churn 50x apart, so the transferable metric is the Scribe repo's own duplication and rework trend over a quarter [R1.14].
- Product outcome: sustained weekly active clinicians plus the drop-off cohort, not "minutes saved"; the time-saved case rests on leader testimony (PHTI, 2025-03-25) [R3.12].
- Review capacity before throughput: under a 2x mandate one company reached 2.09x with merge and revert rates steady while per-reviewer load roughly doubled (arXiv 2607.01904, 2026-07-02; non-causal) [R2.13, R6.2]; time saved writing reappears as time verifying (arXiv 2510.24265, 415 practitioners) [R1.15].
- Error analysis as protocol: annotate 30+ real de-identified traces personally, binary labels, validate any LLM judge on TPR/TNR against a human-labelled set, deterministic assertions in CI, expensive judges async on production (Husain and Shankar, AI Evals FAQ, updated 2026-09-01); their "60-80% of time" is self-report [R3.4]. Fork Omi-Health's medical-note-eval (MIT, 300 synthetic dialogues) as a baseline and TPR/TNR-check its panel first; a competing scribe vendor publishes it [R3.5].

## Sceptics and the foundations gap, with respect

- Open with the number: 29% of review comments are code improvements and 14% defects (Bacchelli and Bird, ICSE 2013). Cosmetic bias is the industry default; calling it this team's failing produces shame and no change [R2.1].
- "This reads like AI slop" is a coin flip: developers with 3-5 years' experience scored 50% (Python) and 49.75% (Java) at telling AI code from human code, and falsely labelled 41.59% of human code as generated (Xu et al., TOSEM 2024). Nobody can do it, so nobody failed at it [R6.10].
- The barrel-file dispute: the missing artefact was the author's rationale [R2.7]. Do not settle it from a blog: Vercel (2023-10-13) concludes barrel files are viable and ships an optimiser, and Atlassian's 75% build-minutes win (2025-06-26) came from a 90,000-file monorepo with build-graph test selection. Benchmark this repo's cold start and bundle size both ways in front of everyone [R3.10].
- The quality instinct is well-aimed though the proxies are wrong: the two quality-focused engineers own the maintainability brief, with duplication, cross-file reuse and swallowed errors as tracked metrics. A job, not a veto [R6.13].
- Foundations rise inside the review path, not a guild: one or two people certify the Effect idioms and every change is authored or reviewed by one of them (Google readability certification, Sadowski et al., 2018) [R2.15].
- Sit-downs stay: 30-45 minutes, off the merge path, the PR chosen for what it teaches, rotating who drives, the author walking through the decision rather than the diff. Justified on knowledge spread, 66-150% more distinct files known (Rigby and Bird, FSE 2013); do not claim meetings are proven not to find defects [R2.6, R6.16]. One a month becomes trace review: 30 real de-identified Scribe outputs, binary failure labels [R3.4].
- Open the AI conversation with METR and its disclaimers together, then the quadrant split, never a demo: claims about speed need a measurement, and until there is one nobody's opinion outranks anyone else's [R6.12].
- Run sit-downs with your own session on screen, including a failure and the check that caught it. Social exposure dominates adoption (skip-level peer use +216% odds of trial; Microsoft, arXiv 2607.01418, measured on Copilot CLI, so the channel is evidenced and the number borrowed). Success criterion in advance: two people other than you volunteer a session within six weeks [R5.4, R6.17].
- Wider rollout: Cleveland Clinic's four pillars as headings, governance, training and onboarding, support, real-time learning (npj Health Systems, 2026-08-11); nothing beyond the abstract until the full text is read [R3.14].

## Deliberately not proposed

- Unattended landing in Scribe or any repo where a bad merge is not cheap and PHI is in scope [R1.16].
- Any third-party PR reviewer without a confirmed self-hosted or in-tenant tier, or before it has been run over a merged Effect PR to count idiom complaints [R5.9].
- Vendor productivity numbers as targets [R1.2], and five claims that failed verification: the Linear "specs before prompts" line, an Anthropic "edges not core" rule, "60-80% fewer rework cycles" for spec-driven development, a 94% root-cause figure for an error-triage product, a DORA "diminishing returns at 10x cost" line [R1.6, R1.13, R5.5, R5.8, R1.9].
- Mechanisms the deployment target lacks: the advisor on Bedrock and the other cloud platforms, routines without a claude.ai login, subagent cache-TTL keys. Team-shaped equivalents: managed settings carrying claudeMd and permissions.deny, a plugin marketplace, path-scoped rules, and the analytics dashboard to test whether adoption is real [R4.16].
