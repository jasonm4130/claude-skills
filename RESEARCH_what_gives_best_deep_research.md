# What Gives the Best Deep-Research Outcomes

A field guide for engineers building a deep-research skill, focused on (a) decomposing a question into logical sub-questions and sub-angles, and (b) tiered verification. Evidence-weighted; claims with weak tier-2 verdicts are flagged inline.

---

## 1. Decomposition: splitting a question into logical sub-questions and sub-angles

The strongest evidence says decomposition beats single-shot generation on coverage, faithfulness, *and* latency — but the **shape** of the decomposition matters more than the fact of it. Three families dominate, and they are complementary rather than competing.

**Outline-first / multi-perspective (best for coverage).** STORM mines tables-of-contents from related articles to discover N distinct writer perspectives, then simulates grounded conversations from each perspective *before* writing. This yielded a +25pp absolute improvement in organization and +10pp in coverage over an outline-driven RAG baseline, rated by experienced Wikipedia editors ([STORM, NAACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347.pdf); corroborated [arXiv 2402.14207](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14207v1)). Both numbers are tier-2 *supported*. The mechanism — discover viewpoints, ground questions in retrieved context, then converse — is the single best-evidenced coverage driver in the corpus.

**Hierarchical / recursive tree decomposition (best for exhaustiveness and recovery).** Two sub-patterns:
- *Ordered bottom-up*: least-to-most prompting decomposes into a sequence of simpler sub-problems where each answer conditions the next, hitting ≥99% on SCAN vs 16% for chain-of-thought ([Zhou et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10625), *supported*).
- *Recursive top-down*: ConTReGen expands a query into a facet tree, retrieves per branch, synthesizes bottom-up ([arXiv 2410.15511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15511)). Tree structure is *supported*, but the specific "planning-then-verification cycle" and "until no further sub-plans" details are tier-2 *partial* (unverifiable) — **downweight those procedural specifics.**
- *Adaptive recursion*: ADAPT decomposes a sub-task further only when the executor fails on it, fixing the core failure mode of static plans (no recovery). Gains of 28.3%/27%/33% on ALFWorld/WebShop/TextCraft are confirmed, but the "vs plan-and-execute baselines" framing is tier-2 *partial* — the real comparator is ReAct and a mix of baselines, not plan-and-execute alone ([arXiv 2311.05772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05772)). The adaptive-recovery lesson holds; the precise baseline attribution does not.

**DAG / dependency planning (best for latency and cost).** LLMCompiler structures sub-tasks as a dependency DAG and runs independent branches in parallel: up to 3.7x latency speedup, 6.7x cost reduction, ~9% accuracy gain vs sequential ReAct ([Kim et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/kim24y.html), *supported*). This is the furthest point on the parallelism spectrum; ReWOO sits in between as plan-first with variable references ([LangChain Planning Agents](https://www.langchain.com/blog/planning-agents), *supported*).

**Plan-first vs interleaved (ReAct).** ReAct interleaves thought-act-observe and can replan mid-flight from evidence, with strong multi-hop and agentic results (+34%/+10% on ALFWorld/WebShop, *supported*) — but it is inherently sequential and high-latency ([Yao et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)). Plan-first architectures are faster (no per-step planner call), cheaper (smaller executors suffice), and higher quality because they force explicit enumeration of all steps upfront ([LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/blog/planning-agents), *supported*). The honest tradeoff: plan-first for coverage and cost; ReAct/adaptive for tasks where the path genuinely can't be known in advance.

**How to measure decomposition quality.** Classify sub-questions as **core** (directly answering), **background** (context needed), and **follow-up** (implications). Across You.com, Perplexity, and Bing Chat, all three miss ~50% of *core* sub-questions. Augmenting retrieval+generation with decomposed core sub-questions won 74% of head-to-head comparisons, and the coverage metric tracked human preference at 82% accuracy ([Xie et al., NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.301/), *supported*). This taxonomy is the most actionable evaluation primitive in the whole corpus — it turns "did we cover the question" into a measurable target.

## 2. Tiered verification: what layered checking actually looks like

The headline finding: **verification works when tiers are composed, not applied alone, and intrinsic self-critique without external signal reliably fails.**

**The floor: don't trust self-correction without external feedback.** A critical survey found *no* major work demonstrates successful intrinsic self-correction (prompted-LLM feedback only) under fair conditions for general tasks; gains appeared only with external feedback — task-specific validators, retrieval oracles, or execution environments ([arXiv 2406.01297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297), *supported*). **Design consequence: every verification tier must touch something outside the model's own draft.**

**Tier 1 — cheap self-consistency.** Sample diverse reasoning paths, majority-vote: GSM8K +17.9%, SVAMP +11.0%, AQuA +12.2% ([Wang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)). The three numbers are *supported*, but the bundled CISC "40% fewer samples" claim is a **citation conflation** — CISC is a separate paper (Taubenfeld et al., arXiv 2502.06233), not the 2022 source. The 40% figure is real; the attribution is wrong. Critically, self-consistency is near its performance ceiling — it extracts near-maximal value from sampling alone ([arXiv 2502.15845](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15845v1), *supported*), which is exactly why you escalate.

**Tier 2 — escalate to cross-model / external verification only when uncertain.** The budget-friendly pattern: run self-consistency first, invoke a second verifier LLM *only* for intermediate-uncertainty cases between two thresholds. This beats pure self-consistency on oracle performance while cutting 13.5–28% of cost and retaining 95% of the gain ([arXiv 2502.15845](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15845v1), *supported*). FINCH-ZK pushes this further: generate semantically-equivalent prompts, sample from diverse models, have a judge classify each sentence-block as ACCURATE/CONTRADICTION/NEUTRAL, then correct at block level — F1 +6–39% on hallucination detection and up to +9pp answer accuracy on GPQA-diamond with frontier models ([EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-industry.139.pdf), *supported*).

**The factoring mechanism (cheap and high-leverage).** Chain-of-Verification's key insight: answer verification questions *without* attending to the draft. Models that attend to their own draft copy their own hallucinations; factoring breaks the bias ([arXiv 2309.11495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495)). The mechanism is *supported*, but "consistent gains across all three tasks" is tier-2 *partial* — the factored variant is not uniformly best (2-step beats it on Wikidata). **Use the factoring principle; don't claim uniform dominance.**

**Compound judges beat big single judges.** Verdict stacks modular units (CoT, JudgeUnit, DebateUnit, aggregation) with *smaller* models and surpasses single large-model judges: GPT-4o Verdict 79.17% vs plain GPT-4o 64.67% on ExpertQA hallucination detection (+14.5pp); even GPT-4o-mini Verdict (67.72%) beats plain GPT-4o; on XSTest safety, 96.44% matching o1's 96.00% ([arXiv 2502.18018](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18018v2), *supported*). **Stacking small verifiers is more cost-effective than one giant judge.**

**Post-hoc retrieval-then-edit beats pre-generation RAG for attribution.** RARR generates queries from the output, retrieves evidence, detects disagreements, and surgically edits only conflicting passages: AIS attribution 35.4→43.4 (NQ), 24.5→31.5 (StrategyQA), 13.2→28.3 (QReCC) while preserving 90–95% of intent, model-agnostic ([RARR](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.08726), *supported*).

**The under-addressed tier: citation faithfulness ≠ citation correctness.** A citation can be *correct* (the doc supports the claim) yet *unfaithful* (the model generated the claim from weights and found a supporting doc afterward). Up to 57% of RAG citations are post-rationalized, which destroys auditability even when citations look valid ([arXiv 2412.18004](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3731120.3744592), *supported*). CiteCheck addresses this with retrieval-first/verify-second: waterfall scholarly-metadata APIs to ground each citation externally, then use the LLM only as a *structured comparator* (0–10 score + confidence + rationale), never as a memory oracle ([arXiv 2605.27700](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27700v1), *supported*). Anthropic's production system encodes the same principle with a dedicated downstream Citation Agent that pins every claim before the user sees it ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), *supported*).

**Which tiers earn their cost:** self-consistency (cheap, high floor) → factored verification questions (cheap, fixes self-copying) → selective cross-model escalation on uncertainty only (medium, gated) → external retrieval-grounded citation check as a final non-skippable gate (medium, mandatory for auditability). Compound small-model judges dominate single big judges at every tier.

## 3. Search strategy: depth vs breadth, iteration, stopping

The consensus is decisive: **adaptive iterative search beats fixed-breadth, and depth should be allocated before breadth, both before expanding token generation.**

**Iteration count.** Accuracy improves up to ~3 searches, then diminishing returns. The budget-ordering policy — add searches first (up to 3), then improve evidence quality via hybrid retrieval + reranking, then expand token generation only for multi-hop synthesis — is quoted verbatim from BCAS ([arXiv 2603.08877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08877), *supported*).

**Retrieval quality is the biggest single lever.** Hybrid lexical+dense retrieval with lightweight reranking produced the largest average accuracy gains across 6 LLMs and 3 benchmarks — larger than more search steps or bigger completion budgets ([BCAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08877)). Tier-2 *partial*: dominance is clearest on HotpotQA and the explicit "neither pure BM25 nor pure dense matches hybrid" isolation isn't in the retrieved text. **The hybrid-reranking lever is well-supported; the cross-benchmark universality is softer than stated.**

**Document count.** Optimal is 5–10 docs for QA; performance plateaus past k=10 ([arXiv 2411.19463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19463), k-range *supported*). The "Llama2-13B declines past k=5" detail is tier-2 *partial* — **that model name appears fabricated** (the paper tested Llama-3.3, Qwen2.5, Gemma2, GPT-4o); the weak-model-decline finding is real, the named model is not. Independently, adding documents at fixed token budget can cut multi-hop performance up to 20% — fewer docs at the same budget usually wins ([EMNLP Findings 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.04388)). *Partial*: the 20% is benchmark-specific (2WMHQA; MuSiQue ~10%) and Qwen2.5 was an exception. **Net rule: cap retrieved docs near 5–10; more context is not better.**

**Adaptive depth, not fixed.** AutoSearch (RL) generates intermediate answers at each step to find the "minimal sufficient search depth," rewarding sufficiency and penalizing over-searching ([arXiv 2604.17337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17337), *supported*). ParallelResearch's tree search deepens a branch only when expected info-gain per unit latency exceeds a threshold (E[ΔU]/E[Δt] > τ); depth 1→2 gives the largest gain, flattening past depth 3 ([arXiv 2510.05145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05145), *supported*).

**Stopping.** Govern by information-utility signals, not fixed budgets: inject Stop when marginal utility falls below δ for m_stop consecutive steps. Agents that self-assess stopping are systematically biased (stop too early when underestimating, overcommit when no useful evidence remains); external utility control yields +9.4pp over outcome-only RL ([DeepControl, arXiv 2602.01672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01672), *supported*). The literature-review "saturation" analogue (new terms yield no new sources; removing a term drops prior findings) has *supported* practical signals, but the "saturation / same authors-ideas-themes" framing is tier-2 *partial* — **researcher-introduced, not in the cited source. Use the two concrete signals, drop the label.**

## 4. What production + OSS systems converge on (and where they genuinely disagree)

**Convergence (strong consensus):**
- **The core loop is universal**: decompose → iteratively search/read → reason about gaps → synthesize with citations. All four production systems (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Perplexity) and all OSS frameworks (GPT Researcher, STORM, LangChain ODR, smolagents, Together) implement it.
- **Separate research from writing.** LangChain's hardest-won lesson: parallel section-*writing* produces disjoint reports ("the section-writing agents were not well coordinated"). Fix: parallelize *research* only, write in a single final pass ([LangChain ODR](https://www.langchain.com/blog/open-deep-research), *supported*).
- **Ground questions in retrieved context before generating them** (STORM's perspective conversations; Perplexity's reasoning pass).
- **Reflection/gap-closing loops** beat single-shot: Together's ablation showed single-step search significantly degrades quality ([Together AI](https://www.together.ai/blog/open-deep-research), *supported*); LangChain's supervisor spawns more researchers when gaps remain (*partial* on the cited secondary source, but the official blog confirms reflection + gap-driven spawning).
- **Token budget dominates quality variance.** Anthropic: token usage alone explains 80% of variance on BrowseComp; multi-agent Opus 4 beat single-agent by 90.2%; multi-agent burns ~15x chat tokens, viable only for high-value tasks ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), *supported*).
- **Reliability via checkpointing + artifact stores.** Anthropic (checkpoint resumption, filesystem artifact store with lightweight refs to avoid "telephone" loss, rainbow deploys) and Google (async task manager surviving device disconnect) independently converged here (both *supported*).
- **Framework design can rival model scaling.** smolagents: switching JSON tool-calling → CodeAgent moved GAIA 33%→55%, a 22pp swing from one design decision ([HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-deep-research), *supported*).

**Genuine disagreements:**
- **Single end-to-end RL agent vs explicit orchestrator-worker.** OpenAI trains one o3-class model to plan+browse as a unit (*partial*: the RL training is confirmed, but the "Plan-Act-Observe/ReAct loop" label is a third-party overreach, not in OpenAI's source). Anthropic externalizes coordination into an explicit Opus-orchestrator + Sonnet-subagent hierarchy (*supported*). Google trains a dedicated planner with multi-step RL (*supported*). No consensus on whether planning should be *learned into the weights* or *architected explicitly*.
- **API vs consumer pipeline.** The widely-cited "three-stage clarify→rewrite→research" OpenAI pipeline is tier-2 *partial* and the **API docs actively refute it** ("Deep research via the Responses API does NOT include a clarification or prompt rewriting step"). That flow is the ChatGPT consumer product, not the API. **Do not design to the three-stage claim.**
- **Fixed-tree vs adaptive search.** GPT Researcher uses a *fixed* configurable tree (breadth=4, depth=2, breadth halves each level, concurrency 4, ~$0.40/o3-mini/~5min — all *supported*). The research literature (AutoSearch, ParallelResearch, DeepControl) argues fixed topology is inefficient and adaptive depth/stopping wins. The OSS default is simpler; the evidence favors adaptive.
- **Perplexity's pipeline** (5-stage agentic RAG on Vespa, 200B+ URLs, sub-document retrieval, multi-stage reranking) is corroborated by primary sources, but the *cited* source is a secondary blog that returned HTTP 403 — **downweight that one citation; the underlying claims hold via Perplexity's own research paper** (sub-document retrieval, self-improving parser, p50 358ms vs Exa 1375ms, benchmark leads all *supported*).

## 5. Concrete recommendations for a deep-research skill

Prioritized. Each marked **[well-evidenced]** or **[speculative]**.

**Decomposition**
1. **[well-evidenced]** Default to **plan-first with explicit sub-question enumeration**, not interleaved ReAct — cheaper, faster, higher coverage (LangChain; LLMCompiler). Reserve ReAct/adaptive recursion for tasks where the path is genuinely unknowable upfront.
2. **[well-evidenced]** Classify every sub-question as **core / background / follow-up** and treat *core* coverage as the optimization target. This is your measurable quality gate — it tracked human preference at 82% (Xie et al.). Production systems miss ~50% of core sub-questions, so this is where the wins are.
3. **[well-evidenced]** Use **outline-first, perspective-guided** question generation grounded in retrieved context before writing (STORM: +25pp organization, +10pp coverage).
4. **[well-evidenced]** Structure independent sub-questions as a **DAG and run them in parallel** (LLMCompiler: 3.7x faster, 6.7x cheaper). **[speculative]** Add ADAPT-style recursive re-decomposition when a sub-task's executor fails — strong gains but baseline attribution was imprecise.
5. **[well-evidenced]** **Separate research from writing**: parallelize research, synthesize in one final pass (LangChain's hardest lesson).

**Verification tiers (stack in this order)**
6. **[well-evidenced]** **Never rely on intrinsic self-critique.** Every tier must touch external evidence (survey arXiv 2406.01297).
7. **[well-evidenced]** Tier 1: **self-consistency** majority-vote as the cheap floor (Wang et al.). It's near its ceiling, so gate escalation on it.
8. **[well-evidenced]** Tier 2: **factored verification questions** — answer them without showing the draft, to stop the model copying its own hallucinations (CoVe mechanism).
9. **[well-evidenced]** Tier 3: **escalate to cross-model verification only on uncertain cases** (arXiv 2502.15845: −13.5–28% cost, keeps 95% of gain). Use a **compound small-model judge** (Verdict) rather than one large judge.
10. **[well-evidenced]** **Mandatory final gate: retrieval-grounded citation check.** Ground each citation externally, use the LLM only as a comparator (CiteCheck), and pin claims with a dedicated Citation Agent (Anthropic). This targets the 57%-post-rationalization failure mode that correctness checks miss.

**Search defaults**
11. **[well-evidenced]** Budget order: **searches first (cap ~3), then hybrid retrieval + reranking, then more tokens** only for multi-hop synthesis (BCAS).
12. **[well-evidenced]** **Hybrid lexical+dense retrieval with lightweight reranking** — the single biggest accuracy lever (BCAS). *Caveat: dominance clearest on HotpotQA-style tasks.*
13. **[well-evidenced]** **Cap retrieved docs at ~5–10**; more context degrades multi-hop QA (arXiv 2411.19463, 2503.04388). Ignore the fabricated "Llama2-13B" specifics.
14. **[well-evidenced]** **Adaptive depth/stopping, not fixed topology.** Deepen only when expected info-gain/latency clears a threshold; stop on m_stop consecutive low-utility steps; don't let the agent self-assess stopping unaided (ParallelResearch, DeepControl).

**Model tiers**
15. **[well-evidenced]** **Strong reasoning model as planner/orchestrator, cheaper models as parallel executors** — converged across Anthropic (Opus orchestrator + Sonnet subagents), Google (dedicated planner), Together (MoA role specialization), GPT Researcher (o3-mini planning).
16. **[well-evidenced]** Budget heavy token spend only for high-value queries (Anthropic: ~15x chat cost). **[speculative]** Adaptively decide single-agent vs multi-agent by whether the brief decomposes into genuinely independent sub-topics (LangChain — confirmed in official blog, but the cited walkthrough was secondary).

**Reliability (if long-running)**
17. **[well-evidenced]** **Checkpoint after major actions; route subagent output to an artifact store, pass lightweight references** to the orchestrator to avoid "game of telephone" loss (Anthropic, Google async task manager).
